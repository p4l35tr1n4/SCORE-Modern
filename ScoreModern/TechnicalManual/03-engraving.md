## Dispatch and object routes

An item enters with its file-order staff context and a working parameter record. The original dispatch at 12DD:0542 subtracts one from the code word, tests it unsigned against 15, and uses index × 2 in the table at CS:0555. Other codes take a separate path. Screen vectors, DAT and print can select different leaves for the same record. Preserve body and attachment order, command prefixes, and carried state. These can affect joins, erasure, clipping, and subsequent items.

## Note positions, stems, ledgers and dots

For the recovered ordinary note path, let `(x,y)` be the prepared head origin, `S` the note's drawing scale after head projection, and `staffStep=f32(staffScale×7)`. The head origin can incorporate cue size, explicit note scale and an offset. Use the head projection rather than the unadjusted P3/P4 point when placing attachments.

- The width used for stem X is `f32(19S)` for the ordinary whole/double-whole head controls 2 and 3, and `f32(17S)` in the common remainder. Only the eligible explicit-stem forms draw the stem. The stem goes on the right for direction 1 and the left for direction 2.
- With `flags=trunc(P9)%10` and `extension=f32(P8%100)`, extra extension is `f32(2(flags−1)−.5)` when there is more than one flag. The length is `f32((extra+extension+7)×S×7)`, signed down or up. A head-specific start factor can shift the stem start. A nonzero P17 remainder modulo 100 overrides the start offset in units of `7S`. The printed hundreds part of P17 selects stem width separately.
- Ledger extents on the ordinary path are `x−f32(5S)` and `x+headWidth+f32(5S)`. Their Y increment is two staff steps, signed toward successive ledgers. The ledger count is zero for ordinary pitches 2–12, and `trunc((1−pitch)/2)+1` below or `trunc((pitch−13)/2)+1` above that range. Special rhythmic codes −2 and −3 take a separate route. The print width depends on the staff control and any eligible P16 override.
- The integer P9 carries dots and flags. Fractional P9 controls dot X via `f32(x+f32((fraction×60+baseX)×S))`. A base of 16.2 applies on the common flagged up-stem branch with zero integer P8. The other ordinary base is 18. P9's thousands and hundreds controls adjust the fractional term or reverse the odd-position offset. P19 adds a vertical adjustment in staff steps. Consecutive dots advance by `13S`, storing X each time. The literal float constants and draw ordering are in the note code.

## Beam, stem, slur and tuplet calculations

**Beam decoding.** Convert P7 to the working signed word. The magnitude's tens digit selects direction, the ones digit counts beams. A negative type selects tremolo, and positive count zero is the floating path. Apply explicit P18 scale when present. The measured cue branch uses the stored .6 when |P4|≥100 and explicit P18 is absent. In beam-scale units, beam separation is 10.5, nominal rise is 49, and head shift is 17. Up-stems shift the starting/ending X by the head offset. Down-stems reverse rise and separation. A secondary P9 packed control can reverse a head-side shift at one or both endpoints.

Endpoint P3/P6 are converted with the factor 150 after any permitted P14/P15 shifts. The normal offset branch uses the stored `.04 × scale × control`; special controls 10 and 20 use opposite signs of `scale × f32(2.83333325386) /25`. Compute left/right Y from their staff projections and signed rise. Thickness starts at `f32(scale×7)`, with optional P17 and the measured .8 cue default, and reverses sign for the up-stem path. The reference slope is `f32((rightY−leftY)/dx)`, with `dx=f32(rightX−leftX)` and the verified zero default of 100. Store the intercept separately. Partial beams, tremolos, and printed-screen differences are expanded by the shape emitter. The stem-adjustment path consumes the beam reference. F2's automatic fit and the AD operation perform their own selection and adjustment.

**Slur.** Resolve endpoint staff and offsets. Store horizontal span, and use the Euclidean length for a sloping branch according to the original packed endpoint tests. The ordinary sampling divisor is 200 when printed and 500 on screen. Explicit trims select 200 unless the packed segment control selects 500. Convert length to the working word before integer division, double the quotient, and start with no fewer than eight samples. The record controls can change the count further. Then `step=f32(length/(count−1))`. X advances with a stored increment on either side of the curve center. The exponent is derived from P9 + 2.5 with a .1 lower limit. For half-curve step `k` out of `N`, the common sampled height is `trunc(peak−(k/N)^exponent × height)`, reflected into the other half. Peak itself is an integer conversion of left Y plus height. The X and Y cap points in this routine are 32760 and 32600. They differ from the earlier generic vector clamp.

The ordinary printed route adds a thickness contour before patterning and trim. Half controls can extend a virtual length before trimming. A straight Code 5 route and ending brackets have separate paths. Tuplet numbers can split a curve around a center gap, derived in the positive-number slur path from scale × 11.4 or 16.2 divided by length. Rotation follows pattern/trim. Preserve those operations in that order. Fractional trims outside [0,1] and reversed trims are compatibility cases. Match a specified mode before comparing them.

## Text, Type 1 and glyph resources

Code 16 stores the text bytes and can store cached alignment/width information. The consumers distinguish width from a glyph outline and from its right extent. The font-number table identifies a PSC assignment. The PostScript FontName identifies the matching outline. Native and Ghostscript text can render the same nominal font, but compare them with the same external resources, font version and metric assignment.

The recovered metric helper computes `initial=size×staff/divisor` and retains it until its consumers. Store `stored=f32(initial)`. The character multiplier is `f32(factor×stored)`; extra space uses `f32(initial×spaceControl)`. This distinction is intentional. Normalize a word from metrics with `f32(word/divisor)`. Compute each advance as `f32(characterMultiplier×metric)` and add the space increment with another store where applicable. Add to the running width with one binary32 store per advance. A final multiplier is stored separately. The primitive adaptation computes `f32(metric×horizontal/primitiveMetric)` without rounding the product first. In the recovered position leaf the character index above 160 leaves the position unchanged. Text control sequences and font state affect which widths the loop visits. The P11/P13 work used by JT is distinct from the baseline positioning and length of the byte string.

FONTCONV parses the AFM `FontName` near the top, and C/WX/N/B fields in the character metrics. The ordinary numeric map indexes by the truncated binary32 difference of character code and 31. The first code −1 switches to the name-prefix sequence. The special scan retains its index and therefore depends on AFM order. Advance uses WX and the right extent uses the third B field. Conversion truncates before discarding high bits. The generated PSC contains a 28-byte padded name and 163 advance words in the first framed record, then 163 right-extent words. The framing uses 0x4B at the start, paired segment-length markers, and 0x82 at the end. The appendix includes the numeric and special mapping arrays.

A DRW bank uses a 72-byte directory frame and a data frame of (signed16 X, Y, operation) tuples. Ten slots are named by five-byte CP437 fields at offset 22 of that directory. The directory offsets are one-based word positions. Blank slots may contain stale pointers. For a numbered file `LIBxy.DRW` with x≥R, the first number is `10 × (26(x−R)+(y−A))`. Slot indices 0–9 add directly to it. Printed glyph definitions on the observed route truncate `6.25×x` and `6.25×y` before writing the integer vertices. That is distinct from the DAT shape's coordinate arrays and scales.

## Vector buffer, raster and output

A point append reads the current count. Where the signed operation input is positive, it inserts a marker word `word(op+0x8044)` into the X stream and increments the working index. The normal coordinate words are then written to their respective X and Y arrays, and the count is updated in X[0]. The original code stores the Y word at a displaced address relative to the X slot. Preserve pointer indexing and marker slots when reproducing DPY, erasure and VJ. A packed marker cannot be projected as an ordinary point. Invalid buffers, original capacity handling, and the user prompts have separate recovery evidence.

The line driver orders endpoints, tests clip boundaries and dispatches to horizontal, vertical, equal-step or general slope leaves. The general clip intersects an edge by multiplying signed differences with a word multiplier into DX:AX and dividing the signed DWORD by a signed word. Truncation, a signed high/low reconstruction and word overflow all occur before the next boundary decision. The clipper uses the actual boundary words and returns branch-specific flags. Raster leaves update Mode 12 graphics memory with their corresponding byte/mask writes. The explicit DOSBox-X results for undefined arithmetic flags describe the measured emulator run. See the indexed flags and fault material for their scope. Console screen state, the edit cursor, buffer replays, and the raster plane are separate state.

For print, compute scale and geometry units from the job, apply origin, visibility and glyph state, and scan signed vector extrema as required by the output route. Default raw origin translation is 2100/27250 in the EPS program. Printed vector emission and the DSC bounding box have distinct transformations. The ordinary DSC box transforms signed extrema with the job scale and rounds each to integer with ties to even, subtracting 1 from left/bottom and adding 1 to right/top. For the rotated scan, horizontal extrema become `−top, −bottom` and vertical extrema become `oldLeft−266, oldRight−266`. The margin calculations also change for this orientation. Mirror changes the engraving transform. It does not enter the measured DSC branch. When a bounding box has been explicitly overridden, or the independent placement utilities contribute their own bounds, use that caller's route.

An ordinary included EPS consumes its bounds for placement. The measured Code 15 program route exposes SCORE parameters as PostScript variables, so record order and external-program state can affect later output. Do not substitute viewer ink bounds for the original vector minimum scan. Native graphics, raster exports, EPS, and a plotted bounding rectangle answer different measurement questions.
